How the CAN control works

The board is tapped into PT-CAN behind the gateway, where the DME, the transmission and the stability control talk to each other. Almost everything it needs is already on that bus, broadcast by the car whether anyone listens or not. So it listens.

What it reads off the bus

FrameWhat it carriesWhat the controller does with it
0x0A5Engine speed, and crank torque with and without the transmission's interventionRPM for the engine-running check and the shift light. The torque pair is how it sees a shift cut.
0x2C7The DME's boost broadcast, the one the gauge usesThe curve's input. Boost comes from the car, so there's no sensor, no hose and no tee.
0x0D9Accelerator pedal, every 10 ms, with its checksumPart-throttle lift gate. Frames that fail the checksum are thrown away.

Two gates the curve alone can't give you

Lift gateNo meth at part throttle

The boost broadcast reads before the throttle, so at part throttle it shows boost the engine never sees. The board holds the pump off under 30 % pedal or 80 Nm of torque, and lets it run again once both are past 40 % and 120 Nm. Lift off mid-pull and the pump stops with you.

Shift cutNo meth while the TCU pulls torque

During a shift the transmission asks the DME for less torque. When the gap between the torque with and without that request reaches 30 Nm, the pump stops; it comes back once the gap is under 15 Nm. No slug of methanol into a cylinder that's just been told to make less power.

FreshnessOld data stops the pump

RPM goes stale after 250 ms, boost after 750 ms, pedal and torque after 100 ms. A signal that stops arriving drops out, and the duty goes to zero until it's back.

When it talks, and when it doesn't

  • It starts every power-up listen-only. The transceiver's silent pin is pulled up in hardware, and the firmware build won't compile without the fix that stops the ESP32's CAN controller sending error frames while it listens. Nothing goes on the bus until the board has heard 200 good frames and the engine is running.
  • It only asks for what isn't broadcast. RPM, boost, pedal and torque are never polled. The one value it reads from the DME is charge-air temperature, five times a second, and only when charge-air trim or charge-air drop is switched on.
  • It steps aside for other tools. Plug a scan tool or a flash cable into the OBD port and the board hears it and goes quiet for 10 seconds after its last request. Bus errors on its own frames drop it to single reads after three and silence it for the drive after six.
  • It never sends broadcast frames. No spoofed messages, nothing the car's modules act on. Two seconds after the engine stops it's back to listen-only.

F56 Code logs through it

The board carries F56 Code's Bluetooth ENET link straight onto PT-CAN, with the OBD port left empty. F56 Code hands the board a log program, up to 40 reads to any module on PT-CAN, and the board runs it at the car, then sends the replies, up to 32 filtered PT-CAN frame ids and its own state every 20 ms back in one compressed stream. Pump duty and current land in the same log as the DME's lambda and timing. DME fault reads and clears work the same way, engine off or running.

Find your own CAN signals

Settings → Advanced → Input has a CAN capture. Give it up to 16 ids and it records every frame of them; leave it blank for a sample of the whole bus. Drop labelled marks while you press a switch or turn a dial, and it writes a CSV with a per-byte summary of what changed around each mark, ready to share.

Spray control

Boost in, duty out. The curve is yours; everything around it is there so the duty only happens when it should.

Curve2 to 10 points

On boost or RPM, straight lines between the points. The F56 default is 25 % at 2 psi rising to 100 % at 8 psi, off again under about 1.5 psi. Edit it on the phone or drag the points on the screen.

Output1 kHz PWM, 25 % floor

Low-side switching through a gate driver and a 90 A MOSFET with a flyback diode. Under 25 % a pump only buzzes, so the board never runs it there.

Soft startNo slamming the pump

Duty jumps to the floor, then climbs at most 25 % every 100 ms, so 25 to 100 % takes 300 ms. It drops as fast as asked.

ArmingArms itself each drive

Self-arming is on by default: start the car and it's ready once the engine runs. Disarm from the app or the screen's Menu holds it off until you arm again or the next key cycle.

TrimsCharge-air and RPM trims

Optional multipliers on the curve's duty by charge-air temperature, by RPM or both, with a cap you set. Hotter air gets more methanol.

StripShift light

A WS2812B strip off the RPM it already reads, with presets per car, four colours and fill from both ends. Latched faults take over the strip, so it's the warning you see without looking.

Before it sprays

  • Armed, with saved settings and a pump current limit set.
  • Engine running over 400 rpm.
  • Fresh boost and RPM from the bus, and the lift and shift gates open.
  • Battery under 16 V and a pump current reading that makes sense.

Lose any of them and the duty goes to zero until it's back. That's a hold, not a fault, so there's nothing to clear.

Pump monitoring and faults

The pump's current tells you what the pump is doing. The board measures it through a 2 mΩ shunt every 10 ms and compares it with curves it learned from your pump.

Three learned curves

Pump test runs three sweeps from 20 to 100 % duty: dry with the tank empty, wet spraying into a container, and with the nozzle blocked. The board saves each one as it finishes and qualifies itself once it has wet and dry. Learn them on water or on your mix. At every duty and battery voltage it then knows what a healthy pump, a dry pump and a clogged nozzle draw.

Warnings keep spraying, faults stop it

A dry tank or a clog can come and go with a slosh or a bubble, so they warn: the app, the screen and the status LED say so while the pump keeps running. Things that mean the hardware itself is wrong latch and stop the pump.

Battery, sensor and current-reading faults clear themselves within 5 seconds once the cause is gone. The others stay until you clear them from the app with the engine off. Under all of it, the pump pins are driven low before anything else starts, both have pull-downs so a reset or a bootloader leaves the pump off, and two watchdogs run.

In the app, a fault shows in plain words on Dash with a Clear fault button, and the fault history keeps the RPM, boost, duty and current at the moment it tripped.

Setting it up

The wizard walks through it in this order, with a tick on every step the board already has.

  1. Wire it. Fused, ignition-switched 12 V and ground, the pump on PUMP, CAN high and low onto PT-CAN, the strip on SHIFT LIGHT and the screen on the USB-C. The car's bus is already terminated, so the board's termination stays open.
  2. Check the boost reading. Start the car and watch boost and RPM come in from the bus.
  3. Nozzle and curve. Pick the F56 preset or set your own points, and set the pump current limit.
  4. Learn dry. Tank empty, 20 to 100 % in a few seconds.
  5. Prime the line. Fill the tank and purge the air out into a container.
  6. Learn wet, then learn clogged with the nozzle blocked off.
  7. Test spray into a jar, judged against the curves it just learned.
  8. Optional features and a backup. Switch on what you want, then save a copy to the phone.

The board

85 × 52 mm, two layers of 2 oz copper. It lives in the glovebox in a printed case.

  • Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 Plus: Bluetooth for the app and F56 Code, its USB-C for the screen, 8 MB of PSRAM.
  • MP1584 buck to 5 V for the logic, the strip and the screen.
  • TCAN1051HV CAN transceiver, held silent by hardware until the firmware needs to talk.
  • Pump stage: UCC27524 gate driver, IPD90N06S4L03 MOSFET, 12CWQ06 flyback, 2 mΩ shunt into an INA180.
  • Sensor input: a divider and clamp on a 0-5 V signal for line pressure or a tank sender, plus battery voltage sense.
  • Shift light through a 74AHCT1G125 so the strip sees a real 5 V data high, its own PTC, and a 300 mA cap in firmware.
  • Default off: the pump needs both the driver enable and the PWM pin high, and both have pull-downs.
