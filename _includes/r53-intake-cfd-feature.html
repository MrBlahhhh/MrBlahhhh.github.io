My printed intake spout against the factory piece

In July I wrote that the factory snorkel shrinks the air tract. This is the number. I scanned the factory front piece, rebuilt its bore, and ran it and my printed spout through the same simulated airbox at the airflow a 240 whp R53 needs.

0.269kg/s of air, about 474 CFM
9.75kPa lost, printed spout
15.99kPa lost, factory front piece
1.94kPa with a 3-inch hole in the rear partition

What was compared

Both parts sit in the same slot in the radiator support and slip onto the same printed snorkel tube. Everything behind them is identical: the tube, the factory lower box, my printed housing and the cone filter.

  • Printed spout. The front piece from my cold air intake tube: a 131 mm slot mouth that funnels into a 66 mm socket, with a snap-on cap. The opening through the cap is 3,816 mm².
  • Factory front piece. The part my spout replaces. Its inlet is a shallow tray that fills the same slot; the air turns sideways through an opening about 24 mm tall into a tube whose bore runs from 1,800 to 2,600 mm².
  • The rest of the tract. The 61 mm printed tube, the factory lower box and the printed airbox with a dry cone filter, out to the throttle body.

How it was modelled

  • One piece of air. From still air above the radiator support, through the front piece, tube, lower box, filter and housing, to the throttle body end. About 1.16 million cells per run in OpenFOAM, steady flow, k-omega SST.
  • Airflow. 0.269 kg/s, what a supercharged 1.6 needs for about 240 whp. That is a target, not a dyno sheet.
  • Filter. A porous layer set to 500 Pa at this flow. That figure is assumed, not measured.
  • Factory piece. Rebuilt from my 3D scan of the part. The scan caught 64% of the inside wall; for the rest I took the outside and subtracted 2.8 mm of wall, the thickness measured where both sides were scanned.
  • Rear partition. Intact, so all the air has to come through the front piece. The last section shows what cutting it does.

The flow

Every arrow is traced through the solved velocity field and moves at the local air speed, slowed 250 times. Both videos use the same colour scale, 0 to 120 m/s.

The factory piece costs about 6 kPa more than the printed spout, and the two tests agree on that: 6.2 kPa in the whole tract and 6.4 kPa on the bench, where each part breathes still air and blows into a straight pipe. Nearly all of it is lost inside the front piece itself.

It is not a bad shape. It is a small one, sized for a stock engine and a quiet cabin. At roughly stock airflow, about 0.15 kg/s, the same bench test would put it near 3 kPa, because loss goes with the square of the flow.

Cutting the rear partition

The airbox leaves the factory partition for you to cut, so the size of the rear opening is your choice. With a 3-inch hole the whole tract loses 1.94 kPa instead of 9.75.

Once the partition is cut, the front piece matters much less: it is only carrying about a third of the air.

What I tried that did not beat it

I also ran reshaped spouts through the same model: a mouth that fills more of the slot, a rounded inside corner, and a bell-shaped lip on the cap. They came out between 10.2 and 11.4 kPa, against 9.75 for the spout as it is. So the spout stays. What does change is the cap: a new one that reaches down to the radiator support and grips the outside of the mouth, which costs no flow in the model. It is not printed yet.

What this does not show

  • No car around it. The air starts as a still box above the radiator support. There is no bonnet, grille or road speed.
  • The factory piece is a reconstruction. A third of its bore is inferred from the outside of the scan, and the lips of its opening are about 0.5 mm thicker in the model than on the part. That could overstate its loss by 10 to 20%. Even then it loses about two and a half times what the printed spout does on the bench.
  • Speed. Above 200 m/s the model is out of its depth, because it treats air as incompressible. The real factory piece would do worse at this flow, not better.
  • Run-to-run wander. The printed spout’s runs wander 3 to 5%, so differences under about 0.5 kPa mean nothing. The factory gap is twelve times that.
  • Not horsepower. These are pressure losses in a simulated intake, not a measurement on the car.
