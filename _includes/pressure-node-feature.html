Pressure CAN node

Six differential pressure sensors and four 0–5 V inputs on one board. It reads all ten and puts them on the car's CAN bus a hundred times a second, so the data logger records aero, intake and cooling pressures alongside everything else.

What it measures

Each sensor has two hose barbs and reads the difference between them, from −10 to +10 kPa. Run one hose to a tap and leave the other open to still air, or plumb both to compare two points on the car.

Aero

Pressure taps on the splitter, underbody, diffuser or wing: how hard the floor sucks at speed and what each surface sees. Dynamic pressure is about 0.5 kPa at 100 km/h and 1.9 kPa at 200 km/h.

Airbox and filter

Vacuum before and after the air filter. The difference is the filter's restriction. A restrictive filter can pull 5 to 7 kPa at full flow, still inside the range.

Cowl and radiator

Pressure at the cowl and on both faces of the radiator. The drop across the core shows how much air actually goes through it.

Four analog inputs

For sensors you already have: oil or fuel pressure senders, a spare MAP sensor, or a tap on an ECU sensor's signal. Each connector carries a switched 5 V supply. Remove its link when tapping a sensor the ECU already powers.

The board

Everything is surface mount and placed by the board house, the pressure sensors included.

How it works

Power

Switched 12 V comes in through a 1 A fuse and a reverse-battery diode, with a 33 V TVS for load dump. An LM5164 buck makes 5 V. Two ideal diodes choose between that and USB, so the board runs on the bench from a laptop and the two supplies never feed each other. A 3.3 V LDO runs the logic.

Pressure

All six sensors have the same I2C address, so a TCA9548A switch puts each on its own branch and the ESP32-S3 reads them in turn. ZERO averages 100 readings from every channel and subtracts them. Press it with both barbs open to the same air before a run.

Analog inputs

Each input goes through a divide-by-five divider (500 kΩ to ground, so it barely loads an ECU signal), an isolation switch that stays open until the firmware is running, a buffer and a 16-bit ADC. A 12 V short on an input only puts 2.4 V on the switch.

Sensor supply

The 5 V on the analog connectors comes off the buck through a 500 mA current-limited switch. It's only there with the car's power on, never from USB alone, and a fault turns it off.

On the CAN bus

A TCAN1042 transceiver sends four 8-byte frames every 10 ms at 500 kbit/s. The IDs and bitrate are firmware settings. A channel that stops updating is flagged invalid in its frame instead of repeating its last value. The 120 Ω terminator is off unless you bridge TERM on the board at the end of the bus.
