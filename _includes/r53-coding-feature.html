02 · The hub

Read. Edit. Write.

  1. Plug in. A K+DCAN cable on a USB-C OTG adapter, or tick the box and use an OBDLink CX or MX+ over Bluetooth. Key on, engine off. An OBDLink, or a cable with no 7/8 switch, needs OBD pins 7 and 8 linked first.
  2. Pick a module and tap Read. The app reads the VIN off the cluster, then the module's coding, and saves a backup before you can touch anything. The file name carries the VIN, the module and the time.
  3. Edit. The full-screen list opens on its own after a good read. Picking a value only changes the working copy.
  4. Write to car. Only the blocks you changed go out, then the app reads them back to prove they landed.

The comms log at the bottom shows every telegram. If something goes sideways, Share log gets it to me.

03 · What it codes

Seven modules.

BC1
Body computer. Lights, locking, windows, wipers, alarm and bulb monitoring. 269 settings on a C05.
EMS2000
Engine. Market, emissions, cruise, and the A/C cut at full throttle.
MK60
DSC / ABS. Sport+, the brake-based limited slip, brake assist.
Airbag
MRS5K, and the MRS4 in early cars. Front, side and curtain bags, belts, occupancy.
KMB
Instrument cluster. Display, units, gongs, and the speedometer number.
EWS3
Immobilizer. Whether the car needs the clutch down to crank.
RIP
Column pod. The gauges behind the wheel, and its own copy of the speedometer number.

Each one reads the index the module reports and loads the table made for it. An index I don't ship yet still gets a full backup, and editing waits until I do. Every table was checked against NCS Expert on the same car: 268 of 268 body computer settings, 76 of 76 on the cluster, 24 of 24 on the DSC.

The convertible top (CVM) and the GT are in planning mode only until I capture a coding session on each.

04 · Edit options

Plain English.

After a read, Edit options shows what the car actually has coded, grouped the way you'd look for it: exterior lights, locking, windows, wipers, comfort. The everyday settings come first and the long tail sits under Advanced. Each row has a plain title, the NCS name, a line on what it does where I've written one, and the current value.

Browsing and editing are free. Writing to the car is the one-time unlock.

05 · Presets

One tap, then check it.

Daily Comfort on the body computer finishes the wipe when you switch off, turns on one-touch windows, the lock chirp, 40 seconds of follow-me-home, the soft interior fade and selective unlock, and stops the cold-filament checks that make LED bulbs glow or warn. Track Day on the DSC sets Sport+, the brake-based limited slip, and turns brake assist and the traction differential control off. Every toggle can be switched off before you apply, and Apply only stages the editor. Nothing reaches the car until you write.

06 · Airbag

Race seats.

Pulling the stock seats and belts leaves an airbag light, and fixing it by hand means finding the right bits for each side in a list of ninety. The race seat screen asks it as questions on one page: which side the driver sits on, one seat or two, harnesses or stock belts, the occupancy mat, the front bags and the curtains. The seat sidebags follow the driver-side answer, and if the module's own left or right setting disagrees, you see that before anything is staged.

Track cars with harnesses and a cage only. This turns crash protection off in software. The battery safety terminal is left alone on purpose; that aging yellow connector is a common false lead when the light stays on.

07 · Cluster and column pod

Speedo calibration.

Speedo reading high? Type in what the cluster shows and what a GPS shows at a steady speed, and the app corrects it. The odometer stays as it is unless you tick the box, and the column pod can be set the same way.

08 · Backups and module swaps

Move your coding to another module.

Every backup the phone has kept shows up by car, module and date. Restore bytes puts a module's own backup straight back, and only when the index matches. Transfer settings is for a swap: it reads the old unit through its own table and sets the same named settings on the new one through its table, so a used body computer on a different index still ends up coded like yours. Anything the new unit can't take is listed, never approximated.

Backups record the car's VIN and the module's own VIN separately. Restoring one car's coding into another asks you to confirm that exact pair. Share sends the backup files anywhere the phone can.

09 · Trouble codes

Read and clear.

Same cable, same session. Engine, DSC, steering angle, body, cluster, immobilizer and airbag each read and clear their fault memory, and the codes come with English names. Chassis modules show BMW's own codes, not made-up OBD-II P-codes. Scan modules on bus shows what answered, and Share report sends the whole read with the telegrams behind it.

Handy after a coding change or a retrofit leaves a light on.

10 · No car needed

Planning mode.

Every option list opens offline, with no car and no cable, so you can work out what you want before you're sitting in the car. It's free and it can't write anything. It also covers the modules I haven't captured a coding session on yet. What it can't show is your car's current value; that takes a read.

11 · Hardware

How it connects.

K+DCAN cable
The proven path. The standard BMW/MINI USB interface, the same one you'd use with INPA or NCS. Every capture behind this app was taken over one. Set its 7/8 switch on. A cable with no switch needs pins 7 and 8 linked at the car.
USB-C OTG
USB-A to USB-C host adapter, so the phone can power and drive the cable.
OBDLink CX / MX+
Bluetooth, no cable. Tick the box on the hub. Same telegrams, same backups. It talks on pin 7 only, so link pins 7 and 8 at the car first.
OBDLink LX
Won't work. Its firmware can't set the parity these modules need. The app tells you that instead of timing out.
Phone
Android with USB host, or Bluetooth for the OBDLink.

OBDLink, or a cable with no switch: link pins 7 and 8

The R53 brings the engine out on OBD pin 7 and every other module (cluster, body computer, DSC, airbag, immobilizer, the column pod) on pin 8. A K+DCAN cable with its 7/8 switch on links the two inside the plug. An OBDLink CX or MX+ talks on pin 7 only, and so does a K+DCAN cable with no switch, so with either of those only the engine answers. One short wire across the back of the car's OBD socket fixes it for good.

  1. Get to the back of the socket. Unclip the OBD socket from its mount under the dash and pull it out far enough to see where the wires go in.
  2. Make a jumper. An inch or so of thin wire, both ends stripped and the bare copper bent over into a small hook so it stays put.
  3. Fit it across pins 7 and 8. Push one end into the back of pin 7 and the other into pin 8, alongside the wires already there. Pin 16 is the red wire, permanent battery. Pin 8 is directly across from it in the other row, and pin 7 is next to pin 8. Keep bare wire away from pin 16: it's live all the time and not fused.
  4. Check it. Clip the socket back in, connect, key on, and read a module that isn't the engine. The cluster is a quick one.

It's the same link the switch on a K+DCAN cable makes, so it can stay in. To undo it, pull the wire back out.

12 · Before you write

Beta, and backups.

Keep an NCS Expert backup of anything you touch

R53 Coding is still beta. I've been testing it on my two cars, and the editor tells you when you're about to write to an index nobody has written with it before. Its own backups are good, but while it's beta keep a desktop TRACE of every module you plan to change.

  • R53 is chassis R50 in NCS Expert. Read with CODIERDATEN_LESEN, then copy FSW_PSW.TRC out of C:\NCSEXPER\WORK\ right away. NCS overwrites that file on the next read.
  • Rename it with the VIN, module and date, like TL22C_BC1_20260726.TRC, and keep it off the PC.
  • How-tos on NAM: NCS Expert for beginners, the R53 add-in and more R53 detail. BMW people: the Dummies Guide flow is the same, pick R50.

If you already know NCS Expert and have an older R53, I especially want to hear how the app's read compares with yours.
